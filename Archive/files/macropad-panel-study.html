Panel layouts for the USB-HID core device. Right hand on the pad, 30 × 10 cm foldout keyboard below. All drawings to scale in millimetres. V1–V4 are unchanged from v1 of this study; V5–V7 are new and each comes in a no-numpad (a) and numpad (b) form.

Three reversals since v1

Displays are back in A screen never threatened USB HID — WiFi does. An I²C OLED costs zero extra pins on a bus you already run. And an OLED bonded to an encoder is a self-labelling knob, which is the one display a layered device genuinely needs.
Buttons are no longer scarce The 25-piece tactile kit (700 Ft) plus topping up the illuminated set removes the two-per-colour ceiling for about 2 700 Ft. Layouts below assume four per colour.
Three encoders is the wall, not two Two SH1106/SSD1306 displays can share the two I²C peripherals at 0x3C. A third display-topped knob needs the TCA9548A after all.

The hand governs the layout

A right hand anchored on the pad puts the thumb left of the fingers. Knobs sit where thumb and index fall; the remaining three fingers fan right and must reach the keys without lifting the palm mid-twist. Every variant keeps that gradient.

V5 · Segment New

Layer and status readout without any OLED. A 4-digit 0.56″ seven-segment display is legible from across the desk at a glance, costs three pins through a shift driver, and never needs a font or a framebuffer. Two 5 mm common-cathode RGB LEDs flank it for CAPS / NUM state. This is the cheapest honest way to make a layered pad self-explaining.

V6 · Knob Deck New

The reversal made concrete. Two encoders wear 1.3″ OLEDs that name what they currently do; the third stays bare. On layer 1 the knobs read VOLUME / SCROLL; on the Planner layer they read MAGASSÁG / KAMERA. Nothing else on a layered device removes as much guesswork per forint.

Three displays is the ceiling without a mux. Two 1.3″ knob OLEDs sit at 0x3C on the two separate I²C peripherals; the 0.91″ status panel joins one of them at 0x3D. A fourth needs the TCA9548A. Note the knob pitch widens to 56 mm here — the OLED body is wider than a bare encoder, and crowding them would make both unreadable and untwistable.

V7 · Full Face New

What the 25-switch kit actually buys: a 5 × 4 key field of twenty positions with no colour rationing, plus an 8 × 8 MAX7219 matrix as a graphic layer badge. The matrix draws an icon per layer rather than a number, is driven over three SPI pins, and is the only indicator here readable from two metres away.

Still not ordering

  • No I/O expander. Every layout above fits one Pico 2 using the diodes you already own.
  • TCA9548A only if a fourth display appears. Three fit without it.
  • No ESP-01S adapter. Your MCU drives WS2812 on one pin; adding an ESP adds WiFi, which is the thing that would actually break the USB-HID premise.
  • Keep the PS2 module on the list — it's for the other project, not this one.
