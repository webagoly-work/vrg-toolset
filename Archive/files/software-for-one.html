Software for one

Personal apps are programs made to serve one person. AI coding has made them cheap enough to be their own part of personal-device software. Here is what the field says, who builds what, and what it means for VRG Mobile.

01The short answer

History

The idea is twenty years old. The cost is what changed.

Situated software (2004), the home-cooked app (2020) and barefoot developers (2024) all say the same thing: software for a small, known audience doesn't need to scale, so it can be simple and exactly right. Vibe coding (2025) made building it nearly free for one person.

Omniapp

The word has no settled meaning.

No source uses it as a concept. The nearest real thing is the super app: WeChat runs its mini programs inside its own web view. VRG Mobile already works this way.

What the serious writing is about

Owning the app, not generating it.

Ink & Switch (2025): AI generation alone doesn't give you control. You also need tools you can reshape and data in your own hands. Sloan (2025) calls the result sovereignty: "it might go away … but that will be our decision."

The one change ahead

Android developer verification, global in 2027.

ADB installs stay allowed. A free limited-distribution account (no ID, up to 20 devices) covers a personal app. A self-updating APK like VRG Mobile is exactly what the rules check.

  1. 2004Situated softwareClay Shirky

    Built for a known group. It doesn't need to scale.

  2. 2019Local-first softwareInk & Switch · Kleppmann et al.

    Your data stays yours: offline, multi-device, long-lived, private, under your control. Kleppmann later called the ideals a gradient, not a checklist.

  3. 2020The home-cooked appRobin Sloan

    BoopSnoop, a messenger for his family of four, built in about a week. "An app can be a home-cooked meal."

  4. 2020Personal softwareMatt Webb (title only)

    The plain name for the category.

  5. 2023Malleable softwareGeoffrey Litt; Ink & Switch essay 2025

    Tools, not apps. Reshape what you have. AI is necessary but not enough.

  6. 2024Barefoot developersMaggie Appleton

    AI lets non-professionals build for themselves and their community, like China's barefoot doctors.

  7. 2025Vibe codingAndrej Karpathy · Collins Word of the Year 2025

    Describe it, let the model write it, don't read the code.

  8. 2026Micro apps, disposable and just-in-time softwaretrade press

    Apps made for one task and thrown away, or an interface made at the moment of need. No hard numbers yet.

What to do with "omniapp"

Search finds only unrelated store listings, nothing on a concept. The closest real pattern is the super app / everything app: one host app with many mini programs in its web view. Take "omniapp" to mean one personal shell that absorbs your small tools, and treat it as our own word.

03The landscape, 2025–2026

The app lives in the vendor's product

Claude Artifacts, Google Opal

  • Artifacts: apps inside Claude. Persistent storage since Oct 2025, 20 MB, published artifacts on paid plans. Viewable on mobile; no APK.
  • Opal: chained-prompt "mini apps", in Gemini as an experimental Gem since Dec 2025. Hosted by Google; no APK.

The fastest way to make something. The weakest on sovereignty.

Builders that produce a mobile app

Rork, Vibecode

  • Vibecode generates React Native through Expo. Rork's recent pages say native Swift and Kotlin.
  • Apple removed Replit, Vibecode and Anything from the App Store. It bans apps that download code which changes their behaviour after review.

Replit, Lovable, Bolt, v0, Glide, Tasker, Shortcuts, Home Assistant, TiddlyWiki: no sourced mobile or offline detail in this run.

What people actually built. Pat Reen's Family Hub, a PWA on a kitchen tablet, mostly written with Claude Code (Feb 2026). An Android Authority writer's household app (Aug 2026): started as a PWA, hit its limits, now being rewritten as native with Claude Code. Christopher Meiklejohn's social app (Feb 2026): built in a week, Go backend, native clients.

The common path is web first, then native where the web runs out: background work, sensors, notifications, Bluetooth. VRG Mobile took that path a month ago, with a native shell and web apps inside it.

05An APK you build for yourself

Android developer verification, from Google's FAQ, read 2026-11-20. Enforcement began on 30 Sep 2026 for certified devices on Android 8+, but only for participating stores in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand. Direct sideloads aren't affected yet. The global rollout is planned for 2027. After that, an app from an unregistered developer needs one of three routes:

Route 1

ADB over USB

Still allowed, with no waiting period. This is what mobile.cmd install does today.

Route 2

The advanced flow

Developer mode, a coaching check, a restart, a one-time 24-hour wait, biometric confirmation. Then installs are allowed for 7 days or indefinitely. Updates of unregistered apps work only with this flow on, or by ADB.

Route 3 · recommended

Register, free

The limited-distribution account needs no ID and covers up to 20 named devices, for testing and personal use. Its launch is unconfirmed: the FAQ says August 2026. The $25 full account needs ID. You can upgrade a limited account but not downgrade it.

A lost signing key can't be recovered, and without it the package can't be registered.

What this means for VRG Mobile. M9's self-update depends on it: an APK that downloads and installs its next version is exactly what the 2027 rules check. Before 2027:

  1. Keep hu.vrg.mobile's release key safe, outside the repo and backed up. Check that BACKUP covers the folder it lives in.
  2. Register the package under a free limited-distribution account once it opens. That covers your phones and the crew's (M33, M35), up to 20.
  3. Keep ADB as the fallback. This is a recommendation, not a decision: question Q6 in PERSONAL-APP.md.
iOS

The PWA is the practical route

A free personal provisioning profile expires every 7 days. EU alternative marketplaces exist (AltStore PAL, Epic, Onside), but Setapp Mobile closed in Feb 2026. For one user on an iPhone, the web app (M36) is what works, and that's what was built.

On-device AI

What a personal app could call

  • Android: ML Kit GenAI on Gemini Nano offers summarise, proofread, rewrite, image description and a Prompt API. Foreground only, supported devices only.
  • Apple: the Foundation Models framework, a ~3B on-device model, offline since WWDC25.
  • For VRG: tidy dictated notes, caption site photos, summarise the inbox, without sending client words to a server.

06Critiques and open questions

  • Maintenance. Sloan's apps are "substantially finished" on launch day. That works for a four-person messenger, not a 34-feature app. Keep each feature small, or the owner becomes its full-time maintainer.
  • Generation isn't malleability. People need to reshape their tools, not only ask for new ones. VRG's answer so far is the catalogue plus requests from the phone (GO VARIANT). Extending that loop is the most "personal app" thing VRG could do next.
  • Security. Nearly half of AI-generated code samples had flaws in Veracode's study. Having one user doesn't make the risk smaller when the app holds keys, client data and a relay into the PC.
  • Consistency. Generated interfaces change between runs. That's fine for throwaway tools and bad for something used every day. VRG's shared kit (Mélyvíz, VRG UI Kit, the long-press menu) is the counterweight.
  • Not found: solid sources on skills atrophy, fragmentation, or who maintains personal software after its maker stops.

07Where it's heading, 2026–2028

Google · plan

Global developer verification in 2027.

9to5Mac · extrapolation

More than 1 million new App Store apps in 2026, a record.

Trade press · speculation

Software made per task and thrown away, "a utility, like electricity". The counter-view (Built In): systems of record keep their value, and only the one-UI-for-everyone era fades.

Not found

Any analyst number (Gartner, IDC) on personal apps.

Our reading

The personal app that lasts is likely a stable shell holding the owner's data and identity, with features made and remade by an agent on request inside it. That's the super-app pattern with one user, and VRG Mobile is already heading that way. This is our inference, not a sourced forecast.
