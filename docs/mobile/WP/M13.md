# M13 · Képernyő (`screen`): the PC's screen, live on the phone

**Goal.** See what is on the PC while away from it: a few frames a second, enough to read the screen and follow a
Planner or Kódváros view. With Légegér's pointer on the same screen, the phone can steer the PC while watching it.
Légegér's 📸 keeps working as it is; this is the moving version of it.

This uses the relay and needs M2 pairing. Traffic is plain HTTP, so on a shared network anyone can capture what is on
screen, client documents included: use a hotspot on a site.

## Measure first

The frame rate below is an estimate. Before building the phone side, run the capture loop alone on this PC and record
the real numbers here: frames a second, CPU, KB a frame at 1280 px. If it stays under 2 frames a second, stop and report
before going on.

**Measured 2026-11-06** on this PC (Intel i5-10400F, 6 cores / 12 threads, three monitors: 1920×1080, 1280×1024,
1280×720), the real capture process from `relay-screen.js` with its notice window on, 12–20 s per run after Add-Type's
~1 s compile, CPU = the PowerShell process's processor time over the run:

| Run | Picture | Frames / s | KB a frame | KB / s | CPU (one core) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1920×1080 busy, every frame, no pacing | 1280×720 | **9.4** | 109 | 1 026 | 68 % |
| same, paced at 100 ms (the shipped cap) | 1280×720 | 9.7 | 109 | 1 062 | 70 % |
| same, low quality (960 px, q 45) | 960×540 | 9.6 | 61 | 586 | 54 % |
| 1920×1080 busy, unchanged frames skipped | 1280×720 | 9.1–9.5 | 109–113 | 986–1 076 | 38–67 % |
| 1280×1024 busy (no scaling) | 1280×1024 | 9.8 | 84 | 823 | 33 % |
| 1280×720 still desktop, skipped, before the idle back-off | 1280×720 | 0.24 (keep-alive) | 15 | 3.5 | 52 % |
| same, with the idle back-off (250 ms after 1 s unchanged) | 1280×720 | 0.24 (keep-alive) | 15 | 3.6 | 12 % |

So: about **9.5 frames a second** at 1280 px, capture-bound (~100 ms a frame, mostly `CopyFromScreen` + JPEG), far
above the 2 / s floor. 55–70 % of one core while the screen changes is ~5–6 % of this CPU; a still screen costs 12 %
of one core and 3.6 KB/s. The first frame arrives ~0.9–1.6 s after the start (Add-Type compiles the C# each time).

One finding on the way: **every PowerShell child started with `-EncodedCommand` was killed after ~9.8 s with exit
code 0** here, a trivial sleep loop as well, while the same loop under `-Command` ran to its end (most likely the
antivirus; CLAUDE.md already notes Avast and fresh script processes). 📸 and the clipboard finish within that, so they
never showed it. The capture therefore runs with `-Command` and gets its C# source through an environment variable.

## How it works

- **Capture.** One long-running PowerShell process, not one per frame, keeps a `Bitmap` and loops over `CopyFromScreen`,
  scales to at most 1280 px on the long side, encodes JPEG (quality about 55) and writes length-prefixed frames to
  stdout. It starts with the first viewer and stops with the last. Windows only in M13; on other systems the relay
  answers with a clear refusal, and 📸 still works there.
- **The cursor.** `CopyFromScreen` leaves the mouse cursor out, and Légegér is useless without it. The loop draws it
  into each frame (`GetCursorInfo` + `DrawIcon`).
- **What to show.** `screen` (one monitor, picked in the view; `VirtualScreen` would shrink a multi-monitor desk to
  nothing) or `window` (the foreground window, as 📸 does). Changing the mode does not need a new ticket.
- **Unchanged frames are skipped**, compared on a small thumbnail, so a still screen costs almost nothing; one frame
  every 5 s keeps the link proven alive.
- **Its own connection.** Frames go on a separate WebSocket at `/screen`, binary, so they can never hold up the control
  link. Never queue: while `ws.bufferedAmount` is high, frames are dropped and the newest goes next. A slow phone gets
  fewer frames, not older ones.
- **Frames are never written to disk.**
- **When the capture is impossible** (a UAC prompt, the lock screen, the secure desktop), the loop survives the error
  and the phone says „A képernyő most nem látható", then recovers by itself. Protected video may appear black.

## Who may see it

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| The relay was started with `--shots`, as for 📸 | Viewing stays off unless the user chose it. START → G stays without it. |
| A trusted link asks for a ticket (`screen.ticket`): random 128-bit, one connection, 60 s, sent in a header, never in the URL | The same rule as M12's board. A stranger on the WiFi gets nothing. |
| Removing the pairing cuts the stream at once | `relay-pair.js` already marks the live connections; `/screen` follows the same mark. |
| One viewer at a time | A second one is refused, and the answer names the phone that is watching. |
| The PC shows it clearly: a small always-on-top window, „A telefon látja a képernyőt: <telefon neve>", with a **Leállítás** button | Nobody at the PC should be watched without knowing. The window is part of the capture process, so it cannot outlive the stream or be left out of it. Every start and stop is logged on the relay console. |
| The stream stops when the view is left, the app goes to the background, the phone's screen turns off, or after 30 minutes | Nothing keeps running unseen. The phone offers „Folytatás" after the limit. |

## Build

- **Relay** (`tools/relay-screen.js`, tested alone like `relay-pair.js`): the capture process, the ticket, the `/screen`
  socket, the rules above. `RELAY_FRAMES_DIR=<folder>` plays numbered JPEGs instead of capturing, for tests and headless
  boxes, as `RELAY_SHOT_FILE` does for 📸.
- `kit-core`: the frame header and the viewer's state machine (connecting, live, not visible, stopped + the reason), with tests.
- `feature-screen`: the live image with pinch zoom and pan, keep-screen-on, a monitor / window switch, a low / high quality
  choice, and a small line with frames a second and KB/s.
- **Steering.** A Légegér strip on the view (hold to move, left and right click) uses Légegér's existing sender, through
  `kit`. M13 adds no new way of controlling the PC, and tapping the picture does not click.
- `Catalogue`: stage BETA at the end of the WP.

## Tests (cloud)

Node, `Mobile/tests/relay-screen.js`, with its own port and `RELAY_FRAMES_DIR`:
- without `--shots`, with an untrusted phone, or with a used or late ticket: refused;
- frames arrive in order; a second viewer is refused;
- the source stops with the last viewer and at the 30-minute limit (a shortened limit in the test);
- a removed pairing ends the stream at once;
- a slow client drops frames, and the relay's memory does not grow;
- no frame reaches the disk.

Kotlin: the frame header (short, corrupt, wrong length) and the state machine.

## Status (2026-11-06): BETA, built on H:, not yet on the phone

**What was built**

- **Relay** (`Planner/projects/varler-planner/tools/`): `relay-screen.js` (new) and `phone-relay-server.js` (the
  third 6 Nov note at its top).
  - The capture: one `powershell -STA -Command` process per stream, compiling `VrgScreen` (C# 5) once. It keeps its
    bitmaps, loops over `CopyFromScreen` (DPI-aware, so a 150 % display is captured at its real pixels), draws the
    cursor in (`GetCursorInfo` + `GetIconInfo` hotspot + `DrawIconEx`), scales to 1280 px (high, JPEG q 60) or 960 px
    (low, q 45) on the long side, and compares the scaled frame with the last one byte for byte (`memcmp`): an
    unchanged frame is not encoded or sent, one is repeated every 5 s marked as keep-alive, and after 1 s without a
    change it looks 4 times a second instead of 10. `screen` = one monitor (`Screen.AllScreens`, picked on the phone),
    `window` = the foreground window (DWM's frame bounds, clipped to the desktop; the notice window itself is never
    taken as the foreground). stdout carries length-prefixed packets (frames, JSON notes), stdin `set …` / `stop`;
    EOF on stdin stops it, so it cannot outlive the relay. A failing capture (lock screen, UAC, secure desktop)
    sends `visible:false`, retries every second, and `visible:true` when it works again.
  - The notice window is part of that process: always on top, bottom right of the main monitor, no close box,
    "● A telefon látja a képernyőt: <the phone's paired name>" and **Leállítás**, which ends the stream at once
    (`screen.end pc`, close 4000). It opens with the capture and closes with it; it appears in the stream itself.
  - `screen.ticket` (control link, trusted only) → `{ok, ticket, ttl:60000, path:'/screen'}`, or a refusal: not
    `--shots`, not Windows, not trusted (`trusted:false`), or busy (naming the phone that watches). `/screen` is a
    separate WebSocket (the server now routes upgrades: `/screen` to `relay-screen.js`, everything else as before);
    its upgrade needs the ticket in `X-VRG-Ticket` (once, 60 s, its pairing still present): otherwise 403, 404 when
    the feature is off, 409 while someone watches. Frames: a 16-byte header (`VS`, version 1, flags, seq, width,
    height, length) + the JPEG. While the socket has more than 192 KB unsent, frames are dropped.
  - Ends: the phone closes it, Leállítás, the pairing removed (`untrust` → `revoke`, close 4410), 30 minutes (4408),
    the capture dying (4500), the relay stopping. Each start and end is logged: who, how long, frames, KB, dropped.
  - `/whoami` gains `screen`; the banner says whether it is on.
- **Phone**: `kit-core/Screen.kt` (the frame header, the ticket and socket messages, `ScreenViewer`: Idle →
  Connecting → Live ⇄ NotVisible → Stopped(why), `FrameMeter`) and the new module `feature-screen`
  (`ScreenFeature`): it starts by itself when the view opens on a trusted link, the picture with pinch zoom (1–6×) and
  pan, the monitor chips + "Aktív ablak", "Kíméletes" / "Éles", a line with frames/s, KB/s and the size, keep-screen-on
  while it runs, ■ Leállítás, and the reason plus "▶ Folytatás" when it stopped (not after an unpairing). It stops
  on leaving the view, on `onPause` (Home, the screen turning off) and after 30 minutes on its own clock as well;
  coming back from Home starts it again. Frames are decoded in memory on the socket's thread, a keep-alive is not
  decoded again; nothing is saved.
  - **Steering** goes through the kit: `kit/Pointer.kt` is a `PointerSink` that Légegér registers (its Bluetooth HID
    mouse and the gyro clutch). The strip under the picture: hold the pad and the phone's gyro moves the cursor
    (Légegér's tuning), a finger drag on it moves it too, **Bal** / **Jobb** click. Without Légegér's Bluetooth link the
    strip says so and offers "Légegér ›". Tapping the picture does nothing on the PC.
  - Catalogue stage PLANNED → **BETA**; `Registry`, `settings.gradle.kts`, `app/build.gradle.kts`.
- **`Mobile\mobile.cmd`** 5) / `e2e` also runs `relay-screen.js`; its relay (1) already starts with `--shots`.
  START → G stays without it.

**What could not be verified here**

- Nothing ran on the phone: the picture, zoom, the chips, the meter, the stop reasons, Folytatás, Home / screen off,
  keep-screen-on, and the steering strip (the gyro clutch from another feature, the drag, the clicks).
- The notice window was opened by the measurement runs, but no one looked at it or pressed **Leállítás** by hand;
  the test drives Leállítás through a stand-in source. Its look at 100 % vs 150 % scaling is unseen.
- The cursor drawing and monitor / window switching were not checked by eye in a captured frame (the frames were
  counted and sized, not looked at). DPI-awareness on a mixed-DPI desk is untested.
- The lock screen / UAC path (`visible:false` and the recovery) was tested with a stand-in source, not with a real

- The `-EncodedCommand` kill was seen on this PC only; whether `-Command` survives every antivirus setting is not
  known. The 30-minute limit was tested shortened (400 ms).
- An unpaired phone on the LAN could not be simulated (every test request comes from 127.0.0.1); the gate does not
  depend on the address, only on the ticket.
- The release build (R8 / minify) was not built locally; Actions builds it on the push. `relay-e2e.js` did not run
  (no Playwright here).
