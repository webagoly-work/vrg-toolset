# M61 · Tárgykereső (`tag`): an Apple Find My tag used from Android

**Goal.** The Founder has an **ISYTag Smart Item Locator** (ISY = IMTRON GmbH, MediaMarkt-Saturn's own brand; model
**IIT 1000**, PO ISY 3901, 3 V DC coin cell; the box says *Works with Apple Find My* and nothing about Android). Asked on
2026-11-20: a new **experimental** VRG Mobile feature that lets an Android user use it anyway. Stage **BETA**, labelled
"Kísérleti" on the tile and the screen.

**Second device (2026-11-22): the iTag**, the generic teardrop "anti-lost" key tag (one big button, CR2032; sold as
iTag / iTracing). The Founder asked for it "in the same fashion", so it joined Tárgykereső as a second kind of tag
(D-152) instead of a feature of its own. It is the opposite case of the ISYTag: no network at all, but Android can do
everything with it. See [The iTag](#the-itag).

**Third kind (2026-11-22): a paired device**, first the Founder's **Q71** smartwatch (round 1.28" calling watch, sold
with the **FunDo** app; paired in Android's Bluetooth settings as "Q71"). Its maker's protocol is not public, so it is
followed through what Android offers for any paired device (D-157). See [A paired device (the Q71)](#a-paired-device-the-q71).

## The hard limit, said first

An Apple Find My tag cannot show its position on Android, and nothing in this app changes that:

- The tag's location reports travel through Apple's network encrypted to a key that is created when the tag is paired
  to an **Apple ID** on an iPhone / iPad / Mac and stays in that Apple account. Android never pairs it and never gets the key.
- Pairing exists only in Apple's Find My app. The tag's firmware knows only Apple's network, so **Google Find Hub**
  cannot take it either.

What Android *can* do is everything the tag says over plain Bluetooth, which is public by design (every passing iPhone
relays it; Android's own unknown-tracker alert reads it). That is the feature.

## What it does

Three tabs, Hungarian UI:

| Tab | What |
|---|---|
| **Saját címke** | "Ez az enyém": hold the tag to the back of the phone and it is remembered (strongest signal by ≥ 8 dB, ≥ −60 dBm). Then: the big distance readout with zone and trend, **Keresés** (sonar tick faster as you near it, screen kept on), **1 m kalibrálás**, **Csipogtatás** / Állj, **Utolsó hely** (the phone's own last known place when it last heard the tag, opened in the map app), **Ottfelejtés** (left-behind alarm after 30 s – 5 min, a connectedDevice foreground service), the details, Elfelejtés. |
| **Környék** | Every Find My device heard: type (AirTag / Find My kiegészítő / Apple eszköz), state (gazdától távol / gazdája közelében / párosításra vár), battery, distance, age, the first bytes of its advertised key. Tap one: ring it, or "Ez az enyém" directly. ★ = mine, · = heard at the same time as mine, so certainly not mine. |
| **Tudnivalók** | What works on Android, what does not and why (the limit above), and that the ISYTag's behaviour is unverified until the phone check. |

## How it works

`tag-core` (pure JVM, all rules, depends on `pager-core` for the RSSI filter and the distance model) + `feature-tag`
(the Android shell), as every WP since M7.

### The advertisement (`FindMy.kt`)

| State | On air | Parsed as |
|---|---|---|
| Paired, away from the owner's Apple device | manufacturer data, company `0x004C`: `12 19 status key[6..27] key[0]>>6 hint` (27 bytes); the random address is `key[0..5]` with the top two bits set; rotates ~daily | `SEPARATED`, the whole 28-byte P-224 key rebuilt and checked against the address |
| Paired, owner's iPhone near | `12 02 status x` | `NEAR_OWNER`; rotates ~every 15 min |
| Unpaired (pairing mode) | Find My service UUID `0xFD44` | `PAIRING` — expected, **to be confirmed on the ISYTag** |
| DULT (IETF) advertisement | service data `0xFCB2` | `DULT` — not expected from this tag; parsed so the screen can say so |

Status byte: bits 6–7 battery (tele / közepes / gyenge / nagyon gyenge), bits 4–5 type (0 Apple device, 1 AirTag,
2 Find My accessory — the ISYTag should read 2). The raw record is split by hand (Android's parsed record keeps one
entry per company id). The scanner uses two filters (Apple `0x12` and the `FD44` service) so it keeps running with the
screen off.

### Ringing (`Sound.kt`, `TagGatt.kt`)

LE GATT connection, no bonding (no pairing dialog). Three protocols, tried in this order unless the tag advertised
`FD44` (then that one first) or one has already worked for this tag (remembered on *mine*):

| Protocol | Service · characteristic | Start / stop |
|---|---|---|
| DULT (IETF, Apple + Google) | `15190001-12F4-C226-88ED-2AC5579F2A85` · `8E0C0001-1D68-FB92-BF61-48377421680E` | `00 03` / `01 03` (0x0300 / 0x0301 LE) |
| Find My (FD44) | `0000FD44-…` · `4F860003-943B-49EF-BED4-2F730304427A` | `01 00 03` / `01 01 03` |
| AirTag (1st gen) | `7DFC9000-7D1C-4951-86AA-8D9728F8D66C` · `7DFC9001-…` | `AF` / — (it hangs up when done) |

Notifications are switched on first (CCCD), then Sound_Start; Sound_Stop after 10 s or on Állj. **The DULT draft allows
sound to non-owners only while the tag is separated from its owner**; what a never-paired ISYTag allows is the open
question of this WP. Every step goes to the Napló with tag `tag`.

### Mine, and following it (`Tracker.kt`)

Android has no key to recognise a tag by, so "mine" is learnt physically (held to the phone) and followed by address.
When a rotating tag goes quiet for 20 s, the **one** new tag of the same type and battery level that appeared as it went
quiet is taken as the same tag (**relink**), shown as a guess ("A címke új címet kapott…", *Igen, ez az*) until
confirmed. Any address heard *before* a later advertisement of mine is certainly someone else's and is never taken. A tag
in pairing mode keeps its address, so nothing is followed there.

### Ottfelejtés and Utolsó hely

The service ticks every 2 s on the main thread (the scanner's thread, so the book has one writer): LEFT once when mine
has been quiet for the chosen time (vibration + a high-importance notification), BACK once when it is heard again. While
mine is heard, the phone's **last known location** (no GPS fix started, nothing is spent) is stored at most once a
minute, if under 10 minutes old: that is the "where did I leave it". No wake lock: under Doze the alarm may come a few
minutes late; that is for the phone check to judge.

## The iTag

An ordinary BLE peripheral with a fixed (public) address: no account, no network, no pairing dialog. `tag-core/Itag.kt`
holds its rules, `feature-tag/ItagLink.kt` the held connection.

| What | How |
|---|---|
| Recognised | the local name starts with `iTAG` (followed by spaces), or `0xFFE0` is in its advertised UUIDs; shown as type *iTag*, state *csatlakoztatható* |
| Ring it | Immediate Alert `0x1802` / Alert Level `0x2A06`: `01` on, `00` off (every non-zero level beeps the same). `SoundProtocol.ITAG`, the only protocol tried on an iTag and never on a Find My tag |
| Button | `0xFFE0` / `0xFFE1` notifies `01` on a press while connected (CCCD switched on): **Telefonkereső** rings the phone (the alarm sound, so it rings on silent too; vibration; a notification with *Megvan*), the next press or *Megvan* stops it, 30 s at most. Presses closer than 400 ms count once |
| Battery | `0x180F` / `0x2A19`, read once per connection, shown in % |
| Its own alarm | when a connection drops, the tag slow-beeps by itself; its button mutes it |

**The held link.** With *Kapcsolat tartása és ottfelejtés* on (the same service as Ottfelejtés), the phone stays
connected to an iTag that is *mine* (`connectGatt` with autoConnect, so Android reconnects when it is back). The tag stops
advertising while connected, so the link reports in instead: the RSSI every 2 s (`TagBook.heardLinked`), which keeps
the distance readout and Ottfelejtés working. When the link drops, Ottfelejtés rings after its time, and the tag beeps at
the same moment. Csipogtatás goes over the held link when there is one; otherwise through a fresh connection (TagGatt),
after which the tag may take the goodbye for a loss and beep (the screen says so). Telefonkereső only works while the link
is held.

**The scan.** With the screen on the scan is unfiltered (the iTag can only be known by its name); with the screen off it
keeps the two Find My filters, because Android pauses unfiltered scans then, and an iTag needs no scan in the
background (the link).

## A paired device (the Q71)

FunDo watches speak a closed protocol: no write-up, no Gadgetbridge support (searched 2026-11-22), so nothing
brand-specific is sent. What every paired device offers Android is used instead (`tag-core/Bonded.kt`,
`feature-tag/Paired.kt`):

| What | How |
|---|---|
| Choose it | *Ez az enyém* lists the phone's paired devices (watches first, with their connection state); no holding to the phone, the pairing already proves it is ours. Type *óra / párosított eszköz*, state *párosítva* |
| Presence | connected = near: the ACL connected / disconnected broadcasts, the hands-free (HEADSET) and media (A2DP) profiles and GATT, OR-ed (`PairedLink`). While connected, mine is "heard" (`TagBook.heardLinked`, polled every 2 s by the service, 0.5 s by the screen), so Ottfelejtés rings when the connection has been gone for its time (a short drop is not "left behind"), with the last place |
| Ring it | a calling watch connects as a **hands-free headset**: the call audio (SCO) is routed to it and a beep plays on the voice-call stream for 8 s, from the watch's own speaker (`WatchRinger`; Android 12+ chooses the watch by address, older versions use the active headset). Needs the watch's *Hívások* (calls) switch on in the phone's Bluetooth settings |
| Distance | only if the watch also advertises over BLE under its name ("Q71", or "Q71-LE" etc., `Bonded.sameDevice`; generic names like "Smart Watch" are never matched); with the screen on |
| Not possible | the watch's own *find phone*, its notifications, battery: FunDo's closed protocol (the FunDo app keeps doing those) |

## Where the facts come from

- AirGuard (TU Darmstadt SEEMOO, Android unknown-tracker detection, **Apache-2.0**):
  `app/.../database/models/device/types/AppleFindMy.kt` (the three sound protocols' UUIDs and bytes, the status byte's
  battery bits) and `DeviceManager.kt` (the type bits). Read 2026-11-20 from GitHub; **no code copied**, the constants
  are protocol facts and `tag-core` is written here.
- The DULT accessory protocol (IETF draft, Apple + Google): non-owner service UUID, Sound_Start 0x0300, the separated-only
  rule and the 5–30 s play time, via search results quoting the draft and Nordic's nRF Connect SDK DULT docs. The IETF
  site is blocked from the cloud session; worth reading the current draft on the PC.
- OpenHaystack / the PoPETs 2021 paper (Heinrich et al., "Who Can *Find My* Devices?"): the full advertisement layout
  and the address = key bytes rule (from the paper as known; the README itself has no byte layout).
- The photos of the tag and its box (2026-11-20), and of the iTag (2026-11-22).
- Q71: a seller listing (uheadset.com, "Q71 full round touch screen … smart watch … with Fundo app": 1.28" 240×240,
  find phone in the FunDo app). FunDo's protocol: nothing public found (searches 2026-11-22; Gadgetbridge has no
  FunDo driver). The rest is Android's own Bluetooth and audio API.
- iTag: `mcauser/micropython-aioble-itag` (**MIT**, README and `examples/alert.py`, `button.py`, `scan.py`: the name,
  Immediate Alert 01 / 00 and "every non-zero level beeps the same", the button notifying 01 on 0xFFE1, the battery, the
  slow beep after a disconnect muted by the button, PCB v1.6 / v1.8 / v5.0 with an ST17H26) and the GATT dump in gist
  `sash13/9dded88b135944cc6259a3eae792c82e` (the same handle layout on an older tag). Not found in any source: a Link
  Loss service (0x1803), whether a phone's own disconnect also makes it beep, an idle disconnect. No code copied.

## Decisions

- **D-150** (taken while building, revisit freely): build for both states of the tag — never paired (pairing mode) and
  paired to someone's Apple ID — with no Apple account anywhere in VRG Mobile.
- **D-157** (taken while building, 2026-11-22): the Q71 (and any paired device) is the third kind of item in the same
  feature, followed through Android's own Bluetooth (connection = presence) and rung through hands-free audio; no
  FunDo protocol (undocumented, not guessed).
- **D-152** (taken while building, 2026-11-22): the iTag joins Tárgykereső as a second kind of tag, not a feature of
  its own; its connection is held only while *Kapcsolat tartása és ottfelejtés* is on (an open link every time the screen
  is opened would make the tag beep each time the screen closes).
- **D-151 = A** (the Founder, 2026-11-22): no remote location. The tag stays local, as built; no Apple account on the PC,
  no key export. See `docs/mobile/DECISIONS.md`.

## What could not be verified here

`feature-tag` was written in a cloud session, where Google's Maven (the Android SDK) cannot be fetched, so it has **not
been compiled** here: the first Actions run on the push is its compile check. Nothing has run on a phone. Above all
**the ISYTag itself is unverified**: whether an unpaired one advertises `FD44` and for how long, its type bits, which
sound protocol it offers and whether it plays for a non-owner, and its signal at 1 m.

## Telefonon ellenőrizni (Android 10+, the ISYTag; an iPhone only for step 9)

1. Tárgykereső → Engedélyezés: the Bluetooth permission is asked; the radio reads "hallgat".
2. Take the tag out of the box, pull its battery tab (or press its button). Környék within ~30 s: a row
   *párosításra vár*. Note how long it keeps advertising before it goes quiet (the pairing window).
3. Saját címke: hold it to the back of the phone → "Megvan", name it, **Ez az enyém**. Walk away and back: the readout
   and the arrow follow; **1 m kalibrálás** succeeds.
4. **Csipogtatás** on the unpaired tag: does it sound? Napló → `tag`: which protocol was chosen (`protocol …`), the
   services listed, any `answer …` bytes. Note all of it here.
5. **Keresés**: hide the tag in another room, find it by the tick.
6. **Ottfelejtés** at 30 s: leave the tag, walk off with the phone screen-off in a pocket: the vibration and
   *Lemaradt* arrive (note the delay); come back: the notification goes.
7. **Utolsó hely** (allow location): after step 6, *Megnyitás térképen* opens near where it was left.
8. Restart the app: the tag, its name, the 1 m level and the sound protocol are remembered.
9. *Optional, needs an iPhone:* pair the tag in Apple's Find My app, then take it away from the iPhone. Környék shows
   it *gazdától távol*, type *Find My kiegészítő*, with a key; Csipogtatás works (DULT allows it now); leave it a day:
   the relink follows the new address, *Igen, ez az* confirms. Note whether Android shows its own unknown-tracker alert.
10. A day with Ottfelejtés on: battery cost (note it here).

**The iTag** (any Android 9+ phone):

11. Switch it on (hold the button: two beeps). Környék: a row *iTag · csatlakoztatható* within a few seconds (screen on).
    If not: is another app (iTracing) connected to it? Close that app.
12. Saját címke → Elfelejtés (if the ISYTag is bound), then hold the iTag to the phone → *Ez az enyém*: named *iTag*.
13. Csipogtatás without the link: it beeps ~10 s and stops; then note whether it starts slow-beeping after the
    disconnect, and that its button mutes it.
14. *Kapcsolat tartása és ottfelejtés* on: within ~10 s *Kapcsolat: él* with the battery %. Csipogtatás now goes over the
    link (the text says so), Állj stops it.
15. Telefonkereső: screen off, phone on silent, press the tag's button: the phone rings with the alarm sound and
    vibrates; press again: it stops. Once more and *Megvan* on the notification; once more and let it time out (30 s).
16. Walk away until the link drops: the tag slow-beeps, the phone's *Lemaradt* comes after the chosen time; walk back:
    *Kapcsolat: él* again by itself (autoConnect), the notification goes.
17. Note the Napló (`tag`) lines for steps 13–16: the services listed, `itag button`, the states.

**The Q71 watch** (paired to the phone as "Q71", calls switched on in its Bluetooth settings):

18. Saját címke → Elfelejtés (if another tag is bound). *Vagy egy párosított eszköz*: Q71 is listed (first, as *óra*),
    *kapcsolódva*. *Ez az enyém*: named "Q71" (or what was typed).
19. The panel says *Kapcsolódva, hívás-hangként is*. **Csipogtatás az órán**: beeps come from the watch's speaker for
    ~8 s; *Állj* stops them; a phone call or music afterwards sounds normal (the audio route is given back).
20. If the panel says *nem hívás-hangként*: switch *Hívások* on for Q71 in the phone's Bluetooth settings, then try 19.
21. Ottfelejtés at 1 perc: leave the watch, walk off: *Lemaradt: Q71* after ~1 min past the moment the connection
    dropped (note the delay); come back: it reconnects (the watch's own doing) and the notification goes.
22. Screen on near the watch: is there a distance (does the Q71 advertise over BLE as "Q71…")? Note the name seen.
23. Napló (`tag`): the `acl … CONNECTED / DISCONNECTED` lines and `watch ring`.
